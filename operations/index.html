Operations

Nobody chose a bad process, and nobody chose for a bandaid fix to become permanent. What happens is that a business grows, the work gets done the way it got done last time, and nobody owns the question of whether it still makes sense. Then somebody buys software to fix it, configures a third of it, and the old process survives inside the new system.

Most of what is broken was never designed

I have spent two decades on the inside of that. Manufacturing, distribution, construction, real estate. Running the operation, owning the P&L, and administering the system at the same time. That combination is the reason I am useful: I am not a software consultant who has never run a department, and I am not a manager who has to ask IT what the system can do.

And I build the fix, I do not hand you a report about it. The deliverable is a working system in your business, not a set of recommendations for someone else to implement.

What the work looks like after that

Systems management

Getting the most out of what you are already paying for.

Read more

It is rare that a business is fully using the software it already owns. You bought it to solve one problem, without realizing what else it came with, and the rest of the capability has been sitting there ever since.

You call it Square, QuickBooks or Salesforce, and it is actually a complete system: a point of sale, a customer database and an inventory system. There are features on your bill nobody has switched on, and there is probably a second subscription doing a job the first one already does. In some cases, using one package properly lets you cancel one to three others.

I audit what you already own, find what you are paying for and not using, and configure it properly. If you genuinely need something new I will tell you, but that is the last resort and not the opening pitch.

Process, workflows and SOPs

Writing down how the work actually gets done, then fixing the parts that do not.

Read more

Not how everyone believes it gets done. How it actually happens, including the workaround somebody invented three years ago that everyone now depends on.

This brings the invisible to the front and gives everyone a chance to help streamline the business. From how a workspace is organized to where a handoff drops, a proper workflow leads to better processes, less waste, and it inevitably reaches your bottom line.

Then it gets written down, so it survives a person leaving, training somebody new, or a busy week. This is where most of the quiet money is. Not in a tool, in the handoffs between people.

Automation, AI and custom applications

Building the thing that does the repetitive work, or the tool that does not exist yet.

Read more

If a person is doing something repetitive, predictable and rule based, that is a candidate.

From a full AI application down to an API call connecting two systems that have never spoken to each other, technology can take out workload, human error and the gaps where nobody can see what is happening. Some of it is a workflow inside software you already own. Some of it is a small custom application built for exactly your business. Some of it is genuinely better handled by AI now than it was two years ago, and some of it is not, and I will be straight with you about which is which. Most of the time AI is the wrong fit, especially when token usage costs you money and a script does the same thing for free.

Not everyone is comfortable with scripts, APIs and AI, and that is okay. That is what you are contracting me for. I build these myself, so you are not being sold a project that gets handed to a development shop.
